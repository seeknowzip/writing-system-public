# Human copy quality

Use this reference for `landing`, `marketing`, `editorial`, and `social`, or whenever the
request explicitly asks for natural, appealing, human, or non-AI-sounding copy. It governs
the creative direction before drafting and the quality judgment after drafting. Fact,
protection, and compliance contracts still govern every claim.

## 1. Set the direction before the outline

Write a private four-line direction. Do not include it in the delivered copy.

- **Reader tension**: the situation, felt friction, and choice the reader is facing now.
- **Desired shift**: what should become easier, clearer, safer, or more desirable.
- **Positioning**: why this offer or point of view matters compared with the current
  workaround, default choice, or credible alternative.
- **Human move**: one suitable expressive move such as a concrete scene, customer phrase,
  contrast, reframe, or purposeful rhythm.

Derive claims from the fact ledger. A creative direction may be a drafting hypothesis, but
it may not introduce a new fact, result, customer quote, emotion, or superiority claim. If
customer language or a meaningful distinction is missing, use plain language and record the
gap instead of inventing research.

## 2. Learn a move, not a sentence

From the user's supplied references, when available, identify one move that fits the current brief. References are optional; otherwise use the genre's information roles:

- turn a feature into a recognizable use moment;
- reframe the category around the result the reader wants;
- use a real customer phrase or question as the entry point;
- create contrast between the current friction and the changed state;
- compress several facts with rhythm when the genre benefits from it.

Apply the move to the current facts in new language. Do not import the source company's
metaphor, syntax, product language, or emotional claim. Do not force a move when a direct
sentence is stronger.

Rhythm, parallelism, compression, and repetition are allowed in persuasive and brand copy
when each part adds meaning. They are failures only when the form is carrying empty or
duplicated content.

## 3. Run the human-quality pass

Before the five judgment tests, run the mechanical checklist in
[`surface-tells.md`](surface-tells.md) once. Diagnose actual loss of clarity rather than banning grammatical forms.

After the checklist, test the draft as a reader would:

1. **Swap test**: could another company or product name replace this one without changing
   the copy? If yes, the point of view or distinction is missing.
2. **Judgment test**: does the copy make one useful choice about what matters, or only state
   agreeable generalities?
3. **Scene test**: can the reader recognize a real moment, decision, obstacle, or result?
4. **Speech test**: read it aloud. Rewrite translated syntax, repeated sentence frames, and
   endings that land with the same cadence.
5. **Memory test**: after one read, is there one concrete idea worth remembering? Do not add
   a slogan merely to satisfy this test.

`AI-like`, `generic`, `flat`, and `not human` are valid symptoms, not complete diagnoses.
Map them to `FT12`-`FT14`, identify the evidence span, and repair the cause.

## 4. Rewrite boundary

- If `FT12` applies, discard the weak creative direction and rewrite the affected title,
  lead, or full persuasive section. Local polish cannot repair a generic proposition.
- If `FT13` or `FT14` applies, rewrite the affected spans and then reread the whole piece for
  rhythm and continuity.
- Preserve facts, protected strings, conditions, and approved voice through every rewrite.
- A draft with an unresolved `FT12`-`FT14` failure is not self-verified and is not ready to
  deliver.

`humanize-korean` remains a separate optional post-process. It cannot substitute for this
pass, and any later humanization must preserve the validated facts, intended rhetoric, and
structural roles.
